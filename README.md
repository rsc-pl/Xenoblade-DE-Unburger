# Xenoblade 1 Definitive Edition UNBURGER Patch (Switch 1 & Switch 2)

## About the Patch

The Xenoblade 1 UNBURGER patch brings the English version of Xenoblade Definitive Edition closer to the original Japanese game. The patch is available for both Switch 1 and Switch 2.
It started as a patch primarily concerned with restoring character names, enemy names, Arts and terminology, and has since grown into a much more extensive project.

While adapting the patch for Switch 2, I decided to release some of the changes I've been working on over the past year for both versions. I've reviewed all proper names, Arts and item names against the Japanese game, including character and NPC names, and brought the shared terminology into line with my other Xenoblade patches. The update covers both the main game and the additional story introduced in Definitive Edition.

I've already done a considerable amount of work on other parts of the game, including the main story and Bonding Talks, which I plan to make available soon. Those more extensive dialogue revisions aren't included in this patch yet. Even without them, a lot has changed since the previous public release.

Unlike my Xenoblade 2 project], Xenoblade 1 hasn't been fully retranslated from scratch yet. That's still the plan for the future.

## The Switch 2 Version

The existing translation has been adapted to the Switch 2 version of the game. I've compared the text files with those of the Switch 1 release and carried over the translations wherever the Japanese text and context are the same. New or changed text in the Switch 2 version has been reviewed against the Japanese and translated where necessary.

Both versions of the patch are being maintained in parallel. Whenever I correct a translation or change terminology in the shared script, the changes are made to both versions. The Switch 2 files also retain the structure and content specific to that release, so you should use the patch intended for your version of the game.

## Core Philosophy

The purpose of this project is to preserve the original Japanese version of Xenoblade as closely as possible in English. That includes the way the characters were written, their relationships with one another, the setting, the names chosen by the developers and the cultural references in the Japanese script.

A lot of names, including those of minor NPCs, weapons, enemies and items, were changed for the Western release. In plenty of cases the original Japanese already uses an English or another foreign-language name. Replacing it with a completely different name doesn't make the translation any more accurate. The fact that a name has been used in the official English releases for years doesn't make it any more faithful to the original.

The English itself still needs to read naturally. Japanese and English work differently, so a faithful translation often requires a different sentence structure. I try to preserve what the characters say and how they say it, including any information and humour in the original.

The names and terminology are also being coordinated across my Xenoblade projects. When the original Japanese games use the same term for the same concept, I'd like you to be able to recognize that term no matter which of the games you're playing. Where a similar-looking Japanese term has a different meaning in another game, it is translated in its own context.

### Previously Documented Localization Changes

Over the years, people have posted numerous comparisons between the Japanese and English versions of Xenoblade, some dating back to the original Wii release. I've gone through the available documented examples as part of this project. Every confirmed issue I could locate in the game's editable script has been addressed, with the affected dialogue independently retranslated from the Japanese where necessary.

These include discussions from Xenoblade Translation Central, older community discussions and other comparisons of the Japanese and English dialogue. The examples were checked against the actual game files, since even a widely circulated comparison isn't necessarily accurate or applicable to Definitive Edition. Several of the reported issues had already been corrected earlier, while some examples could only be traced to spoken lines for which no corresponding editable text was found.

### Honorifics and Forms of Address

Japanese honorifics such as *-san*, *-sama*, *-chan*, *-kun*, *-dono* and *-sensei* have been restored throughout the game where they're used as actual forms of address in the original, as in my other Xenoblade patches.
The way a character addresses someone can tell you a lot about their relationship, especially when it changes as the story progresses.

When characters refer to themselves in the third person by using their own names in Japanese, I keep that in the translation instead of replacing their names with *I*. It's part of the way they speak.

### Nopon Dialogue

The Nopon in the Japanese version already have a distinctive way of speaking. Many of them use the characteristic sentence tic (*mo*), with further variations depending on the character. The English localization gave them its own speech style, often involving broken grammar and invented terminology that has nothing to do with their original dialogue.

Nopon speech has undergone a dedicated revision throughout the game. Invented expressions such as *Heropon*, *Dadapon*, *Mamapon*, *brotherpon*, *sisterpon*, *grampypon* and *littlepon* have been replaced with the actual words or forms of address used in Japanese. The same applies to generic references to *friends* inserted into dialogue and third-person self-reference that doesn't exist in the source. If a Nopon actually refers to themselves in the third person in Japanese, that is retained.

Their original speech quirks have been preserved, including the *-mo* ending where it's used. Nopon also differ in their use of honorifics, sentence endings and vocabulary, and those differences remain part of their dialogue.

## Installation

Choose the patch made for your version of the game.

### Nintendo Switch 1

Install the patch like any other Switch romfs mod, either on a modded Switch or in an emulator.

### Nintendo Switch 2

In an emulator, install the patch like any other Switch romfs mod, just as you would with the Switch 1 version of the patch.

**Before starting the game, set the region to Japan and the game language to Japanese in your emulator's game settings.** On an actual Switch 2, you'll likely need to set the console language to Japanese for the game to launch in that language, since there's no CFW available yet. In the future, I'll consider forcing this through a patch to the game's executable.

Switch 2 emulation is still highly experimental, so testing may be limited by the emulator itself. Please include the emulator and game version when reporting any problems with the Switch 2 patch.

## Bug Reports and Feedback

If you come across any of the following while playing, feel free to let me know:

- Translation mistakes, missing information or dialogue whose meaning differs from the Japanese script.
- Names or terms from the localization that I've overlooked, or inconsistencies in the restored terminology.
- Missing honorifics and other differences in how characters address one another.
- Typos, misplaced text, clipping or text that doesn't fit its window.
- Technical problems, crashes or issues loading the patch.

If you have examples of localization changes that weren't addressed, ideally include the Japanese text or enough information about the scene for me to find it in the script.

I'm not going to revert restored names or terminology simply because some people are used to the localized versions. Feedback on whether a particular restoration is accurate to the Japanese is always welcome.
